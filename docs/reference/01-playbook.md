# X Growth Playbook for @madhugogineni97

This is the master instruction file for the Social Media project. Read it first on every run, then `02-voice.md`, `03-topics.md`, `04-accounts.md` and `05-great-posts.md`.

## Rule #1: verify everything before it's drafted

MG's standing rule is that every factual claim must be verified before it goes into a draft. That covers every fact, figure, date, offer, price, card benefit and visa rule.

- **Confirm it on the primary source.** That means the official site, the press release, the issuer's T&C or MITC, the regulator, the embassy or immigration site, or the live offer page.
- **Leads are not evidence.** News sites, aggregators, X posts, Telegram and forums can tell you where to look, but they can't confirm anything.
- **Visa and travel rules get confirmed on the destination country's official immigration or embassy site,** never on a travel blog.
- **Every draft carries a `Source:` line** that links the primary source.
- **If it can't be verified, don't draft it as fact.** Either leave it out or flag it as "unverified, needs check" in the doc. Never guess, and never fill a gap from memory.

This rule applies to scheduled runs, on-demand drafts and replies alike.

**Not everything needs a hook.** Quick posts (a basic thought, a reaction, a one-liner, a plain question) are first-class. Aim for roughly 1 crafted post plus 2 quick ones a day. Replies are almost always quick. When MG gives a rough thought, keep it a quick post: fix typos and clarity only, and don't add a hook, stats or a moral. See `05-great-posts.md`.

## Rule #2: understand before you write (for some posts)

Added 20 Sep 2026. Verifying facts isn't the same as understanding them. For *some* posts and replies, not all, test whether a claim actually adds up before writing about it. The model is @dmuthuk's post on the $38T GDP claim: he worked out that it meant ~10x in 20 years, set it against ~2x in the last 12, and asked on what basis. MG's own reply to that claim had the same doubt but no number, and it didn't land.

**When to stress-test.** Only when the item has a number worth testing:
- a big target or projection ("$38T by 2047", "1 crore users by 2027")
- a promise or pitch ("save ₹50K a year with this card", "free for 12 months", "10x faster")
- a price, fee or rate change where the real cost isn't obvious
- a trending claim everyone is repeating without checking

Skip it for quick reactions, personal posts, questions, and news that's just news. Aim for roughly one stress-tested post or reply a day, and never force one.

**How to stress-test:**
1. **Claim.** What exactly is being said, and in what units (dollars or rupees, nominal or real, per month or per year)?
2. **Baseline.** Where are we now, from a primary source (MOSPI, RBI, IMF, World Bank, the issuer's T&C, the live offer page)?
3. **Implied maths.** What does the claim require? A growth rate per year, a spend per month, a total cost to stay eligible. Run it in code, never in my head.
4. **Track record.** What has actually happened so far, or what does the comparable thing usually do?
5. **The gap and the hidden assumption.** What would have to be true for the claim to hold? This is usually the post.
6. **His take.** One sentence of opinion that the maths supports.

Calculated numbers follow the rule in `02-voice.md`: inputs sourced and verified, maths simple enough to say in one sentence, one number in the post rather than a table.

**Politics.** When the claim comes from a politician or official, test the economics only. No comment on the person, party or government's record beyond the numbers.

## On-demand mode (MG sends raw thoughts in chat)

When MG pastes a raw thought, a link, a screenshot or some context in any chat in this project, turn it into ready-to-post content. No scheduled run is needed.

1. Read `02-voice.md`, `05-great-posts.md` and the memory feedback log.
2. **If his wording is already there, improve it and nothing else.** Fix typos and clarity, keep his opinion and bluntness, and return one version. Don't add a hook, stats or a moral unless he asks.
3. **If it's just an idea or context,** give 2 options by default:
   - a **Quick** version (1–2 lines, how he'd say it)
   - a **Crafted** version (hook + take + ending)

   Name the pattern each one uses.
4. If it refers to news, a price or a card benefit, verify it on the primary source before writing (Rule #1) and give a `Source:` line. If something can't be verified, say so rather than guessing.
5. If it contains a claim worth testing (Rule #2), run the stress-test and offer a "does it add up?" version alongside the others, with the working shown in 2–3 lines.
6. If he says "reply" or "comment", or shares someone's post, write a casual reply rather than a post. That means no hook and no recap of the post: 1–2 short lines, like "Man, this looks cool. Hope Apple brings it to iOS soon".
7. Add the character count, and `[needs Premium]` if it's over 280.
8. Keep the chat reply short: just the drafts, plus at most one question if a real detail is missing.
9. When he tweaks a draft, note what he kept and changed so the feedback log stays current.

|---|---|

**Tiers:**
- **Big:** 100k+ followers. These are for reach. Reply early: a reply in the first 15 to 30 minutes gets seen, while one after two hours is buried.
- **Small:** 10k to 100k. The best balance, because you still get reach and the author actually reads replies.
- **Micro:** 1k to 10k. These authors reply back, follow back and remember you. At 100 followers, this tier is the biggest growth driver.

**Claude researches and drafts. MG posts and replies by hand.** Nothing is ever posted automatically, because bulk automated activity gets accounts throttled.

## What each run produces: one Claude Doc

Each run creates a new doc titled `X drafts, <Day DD Mon>, <7:30 AM | 1 PM | 7:30 PM>` with these sections:

1. **Right now** (3 lines max). What his niches on X are discussing this slot, plus any breaking item worth jumping on quickly. Include any item from the fast sources sweep that the niche hasn't covered well yet, along with the catch.
2. **Post options** (3 drafts). Each is a different *kind* of post (see mix rules), and at least one is a quick post. Tag each draft `[Crafted]` or `[Quick]` and name the pattern it uses from `05-great-posts.md` (e.g. "News + the catch"). He publishes one, or more if he likes. Every draft shows its character count, a `Source:` line with the primary source (or `Observation, no factual claim`), and a one-line flag saying which sentence is opinion. Anything over 280 characters is labelled `[needs Premium]`. A "does it add up?" draft also gets a **Working** block (3–5 lines: claim, baseline with source, implied maths, track record, the gap) so MG can check the reasoning before posting.
3. **Reply targets** (10 per run). The tiers rotate across the three runs so the day totals 10 big, 10 small and 10 micro. Each entry gives the account and tier, a link to the post, how old the post is, a one-line reason it's worth replying to, and a ready-to-paste reply. Mark replies to relationship targets with 🤝. If a reply uses calculated maths, add a one-line working note under it.
4. **Needs your input.** At most 2 one-line questions, for posts that need his real experience. Never invent that experience.
5. **Evergreen bank** (optional). 1 or 2 posts with no expiry that he can hold for a quiet slot.

Keep the doc scannable. No preamble, and no recap of the process.

## How a run works

1. Read the project files and the memory file `/areas/x-content-pipeline.md` (voice rules and post feedback log). Open the previous run's doc and read any comments MG left on it. Treat those comments as feedback and apply them.
2. **Fast sources sweep.** Check every source under "Fast sources" in `03-topics.md` (telecom, cards and banking, deals, gadgets, AI, startups) for items under ~24 hours old. Then check the fast repackagers on X (@mufaddal_vohra, @Ravisutanjani, @ccg33k). If they've posted something MG hasn't covered, that's a gap to fill. For each strong item, find the catch in the official T&C or offer page, because that catch is his angle. Being a few days late is fine when the post adds the catch or his take.
3. **Relationship targets.** Check every account under "Relationship targets" in `04-accounts.md` (currently @PranayCC) and follow the steps there: one reply per run on a fresh post, a follower and follow-back check, and a log update in `04-accounts.md`.
4. **Harvest X** through the built-in browser, where MG is logged in. Check his timeline, the watchlist accounts in `04-accounts.md`, and an X search for each pillar (e.g. `#ccgeeks`, `Scapia`, `credit card devaluation`, `AI India`, `Hyderabad`). Capture what's being discussed, which framings are getting traction, contrarian openings, and breaking news in his niches.
5. **Sweep the news and verify** using the `topic-researcher` skill. Confirm every factual claim on its primary source before drafting (Rule #1). Popular posts are leads, not evidence.
6. **Pick one item to go deep on** (when there's a good candidate). From everything gathered, choose the single claim, offer or trend most worth stress-testing (Rule #2) and spend real research on it: primary data, the implied maths run in code, the track record, the hidden assumption. One deep item beats three shallow ones. If nothing qualifies, skip this step.
7. **Draft posts** using the `x-post-writer` skill, following `02-voice.md`.
8. **Pick reply targets** from posts under about 2 hours old (under about 45 minutes for big accounts, where possible; relationship targets get up to ~6 hours). They should be in his pillars, and somewhere he can add something real. Where a target post makes a big number claim, consider a reply that adds the implied maths.
9. **Create the doc.** If the browser can't be reached, say so at the top, draft posts from the news sweep, and give reply targets as "search these" suggestions. Never invent post links.

## Mix rules

The 3 post options in each run are three different kinds:
- **Event:** something happened. At most one per run.
- **Take or observation:** his view, a specific noticing, a deep correlation between two unrelated things, or a "does it add up?" stress-test of a claim.
- **Question:** a plain, genuine question to a community, e.g. `#ccgeeks`.
- **Practical:** a deal, an unused benefit, a deadline or a setting.
- **Personal:** only when he has supplied the detail.

Each day needs at least 1 question post and 1 practical post. Each week needs at least 2 deep-correlation posts and 2 personal posts. "Does it add up?" posts are for some days, not all: roughly one a day at most, only when a claim deserves it.

Anchor pillars (cards, deals, AI/tech) appear every day. Rotating pillars appear a few times a week each (see `03-topics.md`).

## Reply rules (the growth engine at this size)

A reply is a mini post. It has to earn a follow from someone who has never heard of him.

- **No hooks in replies, ever.** Write it the way he'd casually react to a friend's post: short, relaxed, often starting with "Man," / "Damn," / "Honestly" or just the reaction. MG's example: "Man, this looks cool. Hope Apple brings it to iOS soon."
- **Don't explain the post back to them.** The author and their readers already know what it's about, so skip feature lists and summaries. One reaction, plus at most one small addition or wish.
- Usually 1–2 short lines, often under 100 characters.
- **Add one thing** when there's something to add: a fact, a correction, his own experience, a sharper angle, a rupee figure, or a question back. When the post makes a big claim or promise, the thing to add is often the implied maths in one casual sentence (Rule #2). Swap vague doubt ("hope they have a plan") for the number that shows why.
- **Never** write "Great post", "So true", "This 🔥" or a restatement of the post. No links, no self-promotion.
- Keep replies to 1 to 3 lines, in the same voice as posts.
- Disagree politely when he genuinely disagrees. Thoughtful disagreement earns more profile clicks than agreement.
- On big accounts, a sharp one-liner or a useful India-specific addition beats a paragraph.
- On micro accounts, be warm and specific. Suggest following back good ones and replying to them regularly, because relationships compound.
- **Relationship targets** (see `04-accounts.md`): be a regular, not a fan. Reference things they posted before when it fits, ask questions they'd enjoy answering, and credit their finds in his own posts when natural.
- Never reply to political posts. A post that quotes an official's economic target is fine only if the reply sticks to the numbers.

## Growth levers (track weekly)

- **Niche clarity.** Cards, deals and AI/tech are the identity. Everything else is personality. The bio should say that.
- **Profile.** A bio that says what he posts and why to follow, a pinned post that's his most useful post or best thread, and a clean header.
- **Consistency over virality.** 3 posts and 30 replies a day for 60 days is the plan. A viral post is a bonus.
- **The first hour after posting.** Reply to everyone who replies.
- **One thread a week** on a deep topic, e.g. "every card devaluation in 2026 so far and what it tells you".
- **X Premium** is needed for posts over 280 characters and is widely reported to boost reply visibility. Drafts that need it are labelled.

## Weekly review (Sunday 7:30pm run)

Put a short **Week in review** at the top of the Sunday 7:30pm doc. Cover which posts he published (check his profile), which ones did best and what they have in common, follower count against the ~50-a-week pace, relationship targets (who has followed back, who hasn't), and one change for next week.

Also run the **Creator study** described at the end of `05-great-posts.md`:
- Visit each creator in `04-accounts.md` and note their best posts of the week and the pattern each one uses.
- Update `05-great-posts.md` with new or retired patterns.
- Add a 3-line summary to the Week in review.

On regular runs, when a watchlist creator's post is clearly outperforming, mention the pattern it used in "Right now".
