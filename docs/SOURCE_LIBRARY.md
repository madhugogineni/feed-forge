# Source library

This is the living source checklist for recurring content research. It records
what to check and why; it does not make every source primary evidence. A social
post, news report, or benchmark summary remains a lead until its underlying
methodology or first-party material is inspected.

Last updated: 23 September 2026.

## AI model releases

Check these categories for every meaningful new model or major version.

### First-party facts

- The provider's official announcement and release notes.
- The model card or system card.
- Official pricing, rate-limit, context-window, regional-availability, and API
  documentation.
- Official benchmark table with the exact model variant, reasoning effort,
  tool configuration, and test date.

Initial providers to monitor: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI, Mistral, and
other providers that become relevant to MG's actual tools.

### Independent and specialist comparisons

- Artificial Analysis for its Intelligence Index, Coding Agent Index, price,
  latency, and throughput comparisons. Inspect the methodology and exact model
  configuration before quoting a result.
- CursorBench when a cited post or release uses it. Trace the number to the
  benchmark owner or primary results page and record the model, effort level,
  task set, cost definition, and run date.
- SWE-bench and other task-specific benchmarks only from the official benchmark
  project or a reproducible evaluation report. Do not mix Verified, Full, Lite,
  or privately modified sets.
- Independent hands-on testers and X posts are discovery leads. Their screenshots
  are not sufficient provenance without a linked method or reproducible test.

### Required model-release record

For each model considered for a post, retain:

- release name and date;
- provider and official URL;
- availability and price;
- compared model and equivalent settings;
- benchmark owner and methodology URL;
- score, units, and run date;
- latency or cost when relevant;
- what the result does not prove;
- MG's conclusion or the specific question that still needs answering.

## Consumer technology market data

### Canalys

Monitor Canalys releases and market trackers even when an RSS feed is
unavailable. It is a required lead source for smartphone, PC, wearable, and
related market-share content.

Before drafting, record:

- geography;
- reporting period;
- whether the measure is shipments, sell-in, sell-through, sales, or installed
  base;
- units and percentage basis;
- publication date;
- comparison period; and
- the original Canalys page or report.

Do not reproduce a chart without checking its usage rights. Recreate only the
necessary comparison with a source note when permitted.

### Supporting sources

- Manufacturer investor releases and shipment announcements.
- IDC, Counterpoint Research, and other established market trackers when their
  methodology and period are clear.
- Official India product pages for launch price, availability, storage tier,
  and offer details.

Use multiple market trackers to understand disagreements, not to cherry-pick the
largest number.

## RSS and first-party monitoring rule

- RSS availability determines collection method, not source importance.
- Use a feed when available; otherwise monitor the official newsroom, report
  index, documentation page, or permitted browser search.
- A secondary article or X post may trigger research, but the final factual
  draft should cite the closest available primary source.
- Store the observation time because prices, availability, rate limits, and
  benchmark leaderboards can change.

## Historical, financial, and quantitative facts

Use this lane to find sourced facts proactively, not only after MG asks for a
specific comparison.

### Preferred primary sources

- Reserve Bank of India publications and Database on Indian Economy for rates,
  payments, banking, inflation context, household finance, and historical
  series.
- NPCI product statistics and official releases for UPI and payment-network
  volumes, values, product changes, and milestones.
- Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation for CPI, national
  accounts, labour, consumption, and other official Indian statistics.
- SEBI, AMFI, stock exchanges, and issuer filings for regulated-market and fund
  facts. Use the source that owns the specific figure.
- TRAI reports and releases for telecom subscriptions, data usage, tariffs, and
  service indicators.
- World Bank, IMF, and other original multilateral datasets for cross-country or
  long-run comparisons when the series definition is appropriate.
- Company annual reports, investor presentations, official pricing pages, and
  archived first-party announcements for product or business comparisons.

### Comparison record

For every historical or then-versus-now candidate, retain:

- both dates or periods;
- geography and population basis;
- series name and definition;
- nominal or inflation-adjusted status;
- currency, units, and conversion basis;
- revisions or methodology breaks;
- source URLs and observation time;
- calculation inputs and formula; and
- the conclusion the comparison supports and what it does not prove.

Do not compare figures merely because their labels look similar. If the series
changed definition, use a comparable segment or mark the comparison unsuitable.

## Interesting facts and numbers

An interesting number needs a reason to care. Prefer one of these shapes:

- a counterintuitive scale comparison;
- a price, speed, usage, or adoption change over time;
- a ratio that reveals a hidden trade-off;
- an everyday Indian number translated into a useful per-day or per-person
  frame; or
- a milestone whose denominator and time period are clear.

Avoid orphan statistics, trivia with no audience connection, and a number chosen
only because it sounds shocking. Label calculated values separately from
reported values.
